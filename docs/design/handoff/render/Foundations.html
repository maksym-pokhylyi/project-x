Recommended changes

01

Give amber a darker partner for small text

Amber #B4690E reaches 3.98 : 1 on paper and 4.23 : 1 on white. Small text needs 4.5 : 1, so a deadline written small in amber fails. It can’t be a filled badge either: white on it is 4.23 : 1 and ink 4.14 : 1. Keep it for markers, rules and large numerals, and add amber ink #925207 for small text. These screens set small deadlines in ink beside an amber marker, so nothing here depends on the new shade.

Closes 27 Nov 2026 · 14 days amber ink · 5.76 : 1 · passes
02

Fold success into brand green

Success #1D6F43 sits 1.32 : 1 from brand green. Side by side they read as one green printed twice, not as two meanings. Drop success as a separate colour: mark “met” and “passed” in brand green with a tick, and tell actions from statuses by form (a filled button against a ticked label), not by hue.

03

Add a stronger line for form controls

Line #E3DED2 is 1.34 : 1 on white. That is right for dividers and card edges, and too faint for the edge of an input or tick box, which needs 3 : 1 to be found. Add line strong #857F70 (3.99 : 1 on white) for controls only. These screens already use it there.

04

Keep info blue out of links

Blue links on a white page are the quickest way to look like a government service. Links here are ink with a green underline; info blue #1F4F8F only labels neutral facts such as notice types. It is also exactly as dark as brand green (1.00 : 1 between them), so never ask the two to be told apart by colour alone.

05

Host the fonts yourself and pin the figures

All three families are open-licensed. Serving them from bidfolio.co.uk takes a third-party request off the first page a supplier opens from an email on a phone. In tables, switch on Inter’s tabular figures so pounds and dates line up down a column.
