(a) Receiving, prioritising and allocating jobs154 words · covered, with evidence

Every repair request from the Council’s helpdesk will arrive in our job management system through the same integration we run for Wharfedale & Aire Homes, where it has handled 11,260 orders since March 2024.1 Each order is given one of the Council’s four priorities on receipt: emergency (attend within two hours), urgent (24 hours), routine (five working days) or planned (20 working days).

Our contract supervisor, based at our Wakefield depot 11 miles from the Civic Centre, allocates each job to the nearest engineer with the right trade and van stock. Engineers receive the order on a handheld device, with the building’s access notes and asbestos register attached, and cannot close a job without a photograph of the completed work.2

On our current responsive repairs contract we attended 96.2% of emergency call-outs within two hours across 4,180 jobs in 2025–26, and completed 94.8% of routine repairs within target.3

(b) Out-of-hours emergencies97 words · 1 gap to fill

We run a 24-hour service, 365 days a year. Two multi-trade engineers and one electrician are on call each night and at weekends on a published six-week rota, with a duty supervisor as escalation.4

Gap to fill. Your documents do not say who takes an out-of-hours call or how the job reaches the engineer. Write two or three sentences here.

Each on-call van carries the materials to make safe the faults we are called to most often: leaks, loss of heating, insecure doors and windows, and electrical faults.4 Where a repair cannot be completed at the first visit, the engineer makes the building safe, tells the building manager what will happen next and raises a follow-on order before leaving site.

(c) First-time fixes46 words · 1 gap to fill

We achieve first-time fixes by sending the right trade with the right stock. Van stock lists are reviewed every quarter against the 50 most-used materials on each contract, and our supervisor telephones the requester before attending any job where the description is unclear.2

Gap to fill. No first-time-fix rate found in your documents. If you measure it, give the figure and the contract it comes from.

(d) Measuring and reporting performance115 words · covered · 1 sentence with no source

We will report against the Council’s key performance indicators every month: attendance and completion within target for each priority, first-time fix, recalls within 28 days and customer satisfaction. The report will be issued by the fifth working day and reviewed at a monthly contract meeting with the Council’s contract manager, in the format we already provide to Wharfedale & Aire Homes.3

After every tenth completed job we ask the building user to score the repair. In 2025–26, 93% of 1,034 responses rated our service good or excellent.5 Any score of poor is called back by the contract supervisor within one working day. We have never failed a monthly KPI review.no source
