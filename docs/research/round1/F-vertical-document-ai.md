**1.5 Outbound arithmetic favours niches with at least ~$150 monthly revenue per account.**
ASSUMPTION (typical cold-outbound ranges, not sourced): one person can responsibly contact about 2,000–2,500 new business prospects a month by email and LinkedIn; 0.5–2% turn into real conversations; a fifth to a third of those become customers. That is roughly 2–12 new customers a month, and nothing in months 1–2.
INFERENCE: outbound alone can carry a solo founder to $10k MRR in a year only where each account pays about $150 or more. Cheaper products need a self-serve inbound engine (search, marketplace listing, community), and section 1.3 shows that engine is contested in the swarmed niches.

**1.6 This founder is squeezed from both ends in compliance niches.**
FACT: established COI vendors attach annual minimums of $7,500–$10,000 to their managed and enterprise tiers (3.3).
ASSUMPTION: those buyers, and finance or safety buyers generally, vet the vendor — security questionnaires, sometimes SOC 2, a company they can hold to account. An overseas sole proprietor does not pass that screen in year one.
INFERENCE: that pushes the founder to the small end, which is where prices have collapsed (1.2). Niches where the output is a finished document a professional checks before use — CV reformatting is the example in scope — avoid both the vetting and the liability.
INFERENCE: when the AI is confidently wrong in a compliance product, the customer carries an uninsured loss, a tax assessment or a false federal statement. A solo founder's only real protections are product design (show the evidence, require a human click) and contract terms. ## 3. Candidate 1 — Certificate of Insurance (COI) tracking

*Evidence base: 10 live searches. No page fetched. No practitioner forum thread found.*

### 3.1 Who has the problem, who uses, who pays, who decides

- ASSUMPTION (prior knowledge, not live-verified): the problem sits with any business that lets third parties work on its sites or under its contracts — general contractors (subcontractors' certificates), property managers and commercial landlords (vendors' and tenants' certificates), franchisors (franchisees), event venues (caterers, renters), equipment-rental firms, freight brokers (carriers).
- ASSUMPTION: user is an office manager, project administrator, contract administrator or accounts-payable clerk; payer is the owner, controller or CFO; the decision is the owner's or the risk/operations manager's, often prompted by the firm's insurance broker or by an audit bill.

### 3.2 The recurring job, frequency, pain, trigger

- ASSUMPTION (prior knowledge): at onboarding and at every policy renewal, obtain an ACORD 25 certificate plus endorsements (additional insured, waiver of subrogation, primary and non-contributory wording), compare limits and wording with the contract's requirements, chase deficiencies, file the result. A vendor usually has three or four policies with different renewal dates, so each vendor generates two to four touchpoints a year.
- FACT: the common small-firm method is a spreadsheet with sub name, carrier, policy number, expiry date and notes; "nothing alerts you when a policy lapses". One vendor blog describes a reminder at 30 days and a phone call at 14 days. (https://workhand.app/blog/track-subcontractor-insurance-certificates/, vendor blog, seen 2026-11-13.)
- FACT (mechanism, from carriers): at the annual workers' compensation premium audit, payments to a subcontractor who cannot show its own coverage are added to the contractor's payroll in the sub's class code and premium is charged on them. (State Fund California, https://www.statefundca.com/policyholder/independent-contractor/employment-status-and-your-audit/; Builders Mutual audit kit, https://www.buildersmutual.com/downloads/BMICO_Audit_Kit.pdf; State Farm, https://www.statefarm.com/small-business-solutions/insurance/premium-audit; all seen 2026-11-13.)
- FACT (vendor illustration, not independently checked): bcs cites $168,457 of additional premium from six missing certificates on one $20.8M project. (https://www.getbcs.com/blog/subcontractor-coi-workers-comp-audit, seen 2026-11-13.) I found no regulator- or carrier-published worked example.
- FACT (cited by several insurer/broker pages; state rules vary): failing to complete a premium audit can bring a charge of up to three times estimated annual premium under NCCI-based rules.
- INFERENCE: the triggers to search are an audit bill, a claim against a lapsed sub, a client or lender requirement, or the roster outgrowing a spreadsheet.
- UNKNOWN: hours per week actually spent by small contractors; share of small GCs that track at all; how often audit charges arise.

### 3.10 AI feasibility

- ASSUMPTION (prior knowledge): ACORD 25 is a fixed layout, so insurer, policy numbers, dates and limits extract reliably. The hard part is not on the form: endorsements are separate documents in many layouts, and the certificate itself confers no coverage rights.
- INFERENCE: a confident wrong "compliant" can leave a customer uninsured for a six-figure loss. Mitigation: show the source region for every extracted field, never auto-approve endorsements, require a human click to mark compliant, disclaim reliance.
- FACT: the incumbent's public line is exactly this — extraction is easy, verification is not (illumend releases, 1.1).

## 4. Candidate 2 — Sales-tax exemption / resale certificate management

*Evidence base: 4 live searches. No page fetched. No practitioner forum thread sought before the budget ran out.*

### 4.1 Who has the problem, who uses, who pays, who decides

- ASSUMPTION (prior knowledge): any seller making tax-exempt business sales — wholesalers and distributors (building materials, electrical, plumbing, industrial supply, foodservice, farm supply, auto parts), manufacturers, B2B web stores. User: accounts-receivable or credit clerk, customer-onboarding staff, staff accountant. Payer and decider: controller, CFO or owner. Influencer: the outside CPA or state-tax consultant.
- FACT: vendors position at exactly these buyers ("retailers, distributors, and finance teams" — Commenda, via https://www.taxwire.com/blog/best-exemption-certificate-management-software, seen 2026-11-13).

### 4.2 The recurring job, frequency, pain, trigger

- ASSUMPTION (prior knowledge): when an exempt customer is set up, collect the right form for the ship-to state (state form, the multistate MTC form, or the Streamlined Sales Tax form), check it is complete, signed, names the right buyer and seller, carries a valid registration number and covers what is being sold; flag the customer as exempt in the invoicing system; renew where the state's certificates expire; produce everything in an audit.
- FACT (how audits treat gaps):
  - Missing certificates produce a full assessment on the claimed exempt sales; partial documentation a partial one (California practitioner guides: https://klasing-associates.com/missing-resale-certificates-can-turn-cdtfa-audit-into-sales-tax-assessment/ and https://sambrotman.com/the-ultimate-guide-to-california-sales-tax-audits/california-sales-for-resale-and-resale-audits/ — law-firm marketing, seen 2026-11-13).
  - Defective certificates are treated like missing ones: Virginia upheld tax where certificates bore another supplier's name or lacked the supplier name, full registration number or property description (P.D. 19-101).
  - Certificates gathered after the audit began get little weight (Virginia P.D. 20-58; P.D. 07-91 lists unregistered customers, invalid numbers and dates years after the sales).
  - Assessments are revised only for sales supported by valid certificates (Virginia P.D. 17-158, https://tax.virginia.gov/laws-rules-decisions/rulings-tax-commissioner/17-158).
  - Alabama runs a named "Wholesale to Retail Accountability Program" that issues assessments (https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/faqs/i-received-an-assessment-from-the-wholesale-to-retail-accountability-program-why-do-i-owe-money).
- INFERENCE (illustration, my arithmetic, not a sourced case): $2M of exempt sales in the audit period, 20% without a valid certificate, 7% combined rate → $28,000 of tax before interest and penalty.
- UNKNOWN: a regulator-published dollar example; how often small distributors are audited; average assessment size.
- ASSUMPTION (prior knowledge, a point *against* urgency): Streamlined Sales Tax member states give a seller 120 days after an auditor's request to obtain a missing certificate. That softens the case for managing certificates proactively, although customers who have closed or will not cooperate still leave gaps.
- INFERENCE: triggers are an audit notice or assessment, a CPA's finding, a new B2B web store, an ERP change, expansion into new states after economic-nexus rules.

## 7. Candidate 5 — Driver qualification (DQ) files for small trucking fleets
